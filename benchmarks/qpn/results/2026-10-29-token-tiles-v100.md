## Measurements

All **49/49** candidate cases succeeded, with QPN and W4A8/MMQ
numerical checks passing. Maximum observed QPN relative expert-output
L2 error was **0.000376**, below the benchmark's 0.03 gate.
The 36 original-schedule controls and 13 additional tile alternatives
produced 36 MMQ wins and 13 QPN wins (the original 36 controls yielded
five QPN wins). All 8 new QPN wins were T=29/32 alternate schedules.

Within-run matched expert comparisons: T=29 16-row is **2.338x**
faster than original four-tile QPN. At T=32 the per-expert QPN
improvements are **2.523x, 2.262x, 2.262x**, median **2.262x**.
Against MMQ, 16-row is approximately 23% faster in the measured
29/32-token cases (about 19% lower latency). At 12/16 tokens the
original two-tile QPN is better than 8-row alternatives, so keep the
original schedule there. For T<=8, W4A8/MMQ remains faster.

The original-run control was materially faster at T=29/32 than the
new-run control (different run conditions); **prefer same-run paired
comparisons**, not cross-run absolute latency claims. The identical
fixture-index hashes establish matched input provenance, not identical
GPU clock/thermal state.

## Interpretation and gates

The sharp 17+ token regression is attributable to the old four-row-tile
**single-CTA** schedule: independent 16-row CTA partitions in grid.z
avoid the oversized four-tile work parcel. This is a verified
*standalone per-expert prefill* win, not an engine-wide speedup.
Recommended next standalone schedule hypothesis: preserve original
8-row CTA for T<=8, original 16-row CTA for 9..16, and explicit
16-row CTA partitions for 17..32. **Do not enable production dispatch.**

Still required before production: boundary-tail correctness (T=17,24,31),
CUDA memory/sanitizer and graph-replay checks, multi-expert nonuniform
routing, expert supply and cache behavior, end-to-end full-model TTFT,
and regression/quality measurements. Keep the old schedule as a
measured control. No main-branch or runtime change was made.
